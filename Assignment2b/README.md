# NASA C-MAPSS — FDE Data Foundations Assessment 2B

## 1. Problem Statement

Maintenance and reliability stakeholders require a dependable analytical foundation for understanding longitudinal sensor behavior across simulated turbofan engine lifecycles. Prior to developing predictive algorithms or condition-based maintenance policies, engineering organizations must evaluate whether available telemetry contains measurable signals associated with simulated degradation, establish source reliability, audit data quality, and define transparent boundaries.

This assessment evaluates the foundational data integrity of the simulation before predictive modeling is considered. It does not evaluate real-world engine failure probabilities, project production maintenance cost savings, draw regulatory airworthiness conclusions, or analyze actual in-flight failures.

---

## 2. Dataset

The investigation utilizes the **NASA Commercial Modular Aero-Propulsion System Simulation (C-MAPSS)** run-to-failure dataset, generated by the NASA Prognostics Center of Excellence (PCoE). The dataset simulates gas-turbine degradation trajectories for commercial turbofan engines in the 90,000 lb thrust class.

Each operational trajectory captures:
* **Entity & Temporal Coordinates**: Engine unit identifier (`unit_number`) and discrete flight cycle counter (`time_in_cycles`).
* **Operational Settings**: 3 operating conditions (`op_setting_1`, `op_setting_2`, `op_setting_3`) tracking altitude, Mach number, and throttle resolver angle (TRA).
* **Sensor Telemetry**: 21 continuous gas-path instrumentation measurements (`sensor_1` through `sensor_21`) stationed across fan inlet, compressors, combustor, turbines, and nozzles.
* **Trajectory Partitions**: Run-to-failure training files (`train_FD*.txt`), censored test sequences (`test_FD*.txt`), and ground-truth remaining useful life vectors (`RUL_FD*.txt`).

---

## 3. Scope

The primary analytical scope of this assessment is strictly **Sub-dataset FD001**:
* **Operating Regime**: Single nominal flight condition (Sea-Level Static/Cruise: Altitude $\approx 0$, Mach $\approx 0$, TRA $= 100$).
* **Fault Mode**: Single degradation mechanism (High-Pressure Compressor degradation).
* **Population**: 100 training engines (20,631 run-to-failure cycle records) and 100 test engines (13,096 censored records).

*Archive Preservation Note*: All 14 original C-MAPSS archive files spanning sub-datasets FD001 through FD004 are preserved intact in `data/raw/CMAPSSData/` to maintain data provenance, but analytical modeling and metric derivation are governed strictly within FD001.

---

## 4. Analytical Question

The assessment investigates:
> *"Does the NASA C-MAPSS FD001 dataset provide a dependable data foundation for investigating simulated turbofan degradation, establishing complete temporal cycle continuity, clean grain uniqueness, and measurable sensor variation with exploratory evidence of association with cycle progression?"*

The investigation specifically audits:
1. Data ingestion fidelity and whitespace parsing integrity.
2. Composite grain uniqueness and temporal sequence continuity.
3. Within-dataset sensor variance and detection of invariant telemetry channels.
4. Trajectory lifespan dispersion across the simulated fleet.
5. Formulation of governed operational metrics without premature predictive modeling.

## 6. Data Validation

Data quality was evaluated using a formal failure contract (`PASS`, `WARN`, `UNKNOWN`, `FAIL`):

| Validation Rule | Target Expectation | Observed Dataset Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|:---:|
| **Composite Grain Uniqueness** | Exactly 1 record per `(unit_number, time_in_cycles)` | 20,631 unique pairs, 0 duplicate snapshots in train; 13,096 unique pairs in test. | **`PASS`** |
| **Initial Cycle Invariant** | Every engine starts at cycle 1 | 100 of 100 training engines and 100 of 100 test engines start at cycle 1. | **`PASS`** |
| **Temporal Sequence Continuity** | $\Delta \text{cycle} \equiv 1$ strictly, zero gaps | Zero missing cycles, zero out-of-order sequence timestamps. | **`PASS`** |
| **Sensor Variance Gate** | Active variation ($\sigma \ge 10^{-4}$) across all channels | Exactly 15 sensors show active variation; 6 sensors are constant flatlines. | **`WARN`** |
| **Whitespace Parsing Integrity** | Zero null values across 26 columns | Trailing whitespace trap resolved via `sep=r'\s+'`; 0 NaNs detected. | **`PASS`** |
| **Train/Test Lifespan Parity** | Implied test lifespans match training distribution | Train Mean: 206.31 cycles; Test Implied Mean: 206.52 cycles. | **`PASS`** |
| **Dataset Metadata Discrepancy** | File record counts match `readme.txt` | In FD004, `readme.txt` swapped train/test counts (doc: 248/249; files: 249/248). | **`WARN`** |
| **Operational Decision Context** | Maintenance costs and regulatory safety limits | No financial costs, airworthiness rules, or flight hours present in data. | **`UNKNOWN`** |

*Variance Gate Note*: The 6 invariant channels (`sensor_1, 5, 10, 16, 18, 19`) have $\sigma = 0.000000$ due to fixed sea-level boundary conditions. The gate raises a `WARN` to quarantine these channels from downstream analysis without failing the pipeline.

## 7. Workflow Model

The analytical workflow models observable data states from operational cycle to simulated failure:

$$\text{Engine} \longrightarrow \text{Flight Cycle} \longrightarrow \text{Sensor Observation} \longrightarrow \text{Longitudinal Series} \longrightarrow \text{Baseline Drift} \longrightarrow \text{Failure Endpoint}$$

### Analytical Assumptions & Cohorts
* **Per-Engine Baseline (Analytical Assumption)**: Initial cycles exhibit random manufacturing variation. The pipeline computes early reference baselines from the first 5 cycles (`time_in_cycles <= 5`) per engine to center drift trajectories. This is an analytical normalization assumption.
* **Cumulative Drift Extraction**: Active channels (e.g. $T_{50}$ / `sensor_4`, $P_{30}$ / `sensor_7`, $P_{s30}$ / `sensor_11`, $N_c$ / `sensor_9`) are transformed into cumulative drift deviations from baseline.
* **Exploratory Lifecycle Segmentation (Analytical Cohorts)**:
  * `NOMINAL`: Remaining Useful Life ($\text{RUL} > 60$ cycles)
  * `ADVISORY`: $30 < \text{RUL} \le 60$ cycles
  * `CRITICAL`: $\text{RUL} \le 30$ cycles
  * *Governance Note*: These cutoffs are exploratory analytical definitions established for population quantification; they are not validated airline maintenance triggers.

The canonical output (`outputs/final_output.csv`) preserves exact grain invariance ($20,631$ rows, 0 duplicates, 0 Cartesian fanout).

## 8. Metrics

The project derives five governed metrics exported to `outputs/metrics.csv`:

| Metric Name | Formula | Population / Grain | Value | Status | Operational Interpretation | Explicit Limitation |
|---|---|---|:---:|:---:|---|---|
| **`non_constant_sensor_ratio`** | $\frac{\text{count}(\sigma \ge 10^{-4})}{N_{\text{sensors}}} \times 100$ | Dataset Schema (21 sensors) | **71.43%** | `FACT` | 15 of 21 sensors show within-dataset variation under FD001; 6 channels are constant. | Non-constant variance indicates measurement variation, not physical diagnostic causation. |
| **`lifespan_coefficient_of_variation`** | $\frac{s_{\text{lifespan}}}{\bar{C}_{\text{fail}}} \times 100$ | Fleet Lifespans ($N=100$ engines) | **22.46%** | `OBSERVATIONAL` | Simulated engine trajectories terminate across a wide span (128 to 362 cycles; mean: 206.31, std: 46.34). | Descriptive measure of simulated spread; does not prove maintenance economics or policy superiority. |
| **`critical_lifecycle_exposure`** | $\frac{\text{count}(\text{RUL} \le 30)}{N_{\text{total\_cycles}}} \times 100$ | Total Snapshots ($N=20,631$) | **15.03%** | `UNRATIFIED` | Fraction of training records occurring within 30 cycles of simulated endpoint ($3,100 / 20,631$). | Analytical lifecycle segmentation only; threshold of 30 cycles is not a validated maintenance standard. |
| **`advisory_lifecycle_exposure`** | $\frac{\text{count}(30 < \text{RUL} \le 60)}{N_{\text{total\_cycles}}} \times 100$ | Total Snapshots ($N=20,631$) | **14.54%** | `UNRATIFIED` | Fraction of training records occurring within 31–60 cycles prior to endpoint ($3,000 / 20,631$). | Analytical lifecycle segmentation only; exploratory analytical boundary. |
| **`trajectory_completeness_rate`** | $\frac{N_{\text{complete\_engines}}}{N_{\text{total\_engines}}} \times 100$ | Engine Trajectories ($N=100$) | **100.00%** | `FACT` | All 100 training engines have unbroken, contiguous cycle sequences starting at cycle 1. | Confirms simulation sequence integrity; does not provide elapsed calendar or flight hours. |

## 9. Key Findings

All findings reflect observable dataset evidence:

1. **Measurable Sensor Variation**: 15 of 21 sensor channels exhibit active within-dataset variation under single-regime sea-level operation, while 6 channels are strictly invariant ($\sigma = 0.000$).
2. **Exploratory Drift Associated with Cycle Progression**: Several active gas-path sensors exhibit systematic correlation with cycle progression across trajectories:
   * Upward drift: `sensor_11` ($P_{s30}$, rank $\rho = +0.62$), `sensor_4` ($T_{50}$, rank $\rho = +0.61$), `sensor_15` ($BPR$, rank $\rho = +0.57$).
   * Downward drift: `sensor_12` ($\phi$, rank $\rho = -0.59$), `sensor_7` ($P_{30}$, rank $\rho = -0.58$), `sensor_21` ($W_{32}$, rank $\rho = -0.57$).
   * *Governance Caveat*: Sensor drift is an empirical observation associated with simulated cycle count; it does **not** prove physical causation or component-level fault mechanics.
3. **Substantial Lifetime Heterogeneity**: Run-to-failure lifespans span 128 to 362 cycles with a 22.46% coefficient of variation, demonstrating that simulated engines do not fail at uniform cycle intervals.
4. **Pristine Temporal Integrity**: All 100 training engines and 100 test engines possess complete, unbroken cycle sequences starting at cycle 1 with zero missing snapshots.
5. **Late-Life Volume Quantification**: Approximately 15.03% of total training snapshots occur within the final 30 cycles of simulated operation.

## 10. What the Dataset Does NOT Establish

To maintain strict scientific and engineering governance, the following aspects are explicitly identified as **unsupported by the dataset**:

* **Exact Point of Fault Inception**: The dataset does not annotate the precise cycle where nominal wear transitions to damage propagation.
* **Instantaneous Component Health State**: Intermediate numerical health margins ($m_{\text{HPC}}, m_{\text{EGT}}$) between cycle 1 and failure are unobserved.
* **Maintenance Economics**: The dataset contains zero financial metrics (overhaul costs, line maintenance labor, part replacement expenses, or cancellation penalties).
* **Production Maintenance Thresholds**: The dataset does not define or validate operational alarm thresholds or maintenance triggers.
* **Real-World Failure Probabilities**: The simulation reflects synthetic run-to-failure endpoints; it does not model probabilistic in-flight component breakdown rates.
* **Physical Time per Cycle**: Telemetry records discrete flight cycles without calendar timestamps or elapsed flight hours.
* **Causal Relationships**: Statistical correlation between sensor channels and cycle progression does not establish physical thermodynamic causation.

## 11. Pipeline Architecture

The dependable Python pipeline executes sequentially without circular dependencies:

```
RAW ARCHIVE (data/raw/CMAPSSData/train_FD001.txt, test_FD001.txt, RUL_FD001.txt)
   ↓
INGESTION (pipeline/ingest.py: whitespace regex parsing, schema typing, zero NaNs)
   ↓
VALIDATION (pipeline/validate.py: grain audit, cycle continuity, flatline sensor gate)
   ↓
TRANSFORMATION (pipeline/transform.py: 5-cycle baselines, cumulative drift, wear index)
   ↓
METRICS (pipeline/metrics.py: 5 governed operational metrics with governance tagging)
   ↓
REPRODUCIBLE OUTPUTS (outputs/final_output.csv, metrics.csv, validation_report.json, log)
```
